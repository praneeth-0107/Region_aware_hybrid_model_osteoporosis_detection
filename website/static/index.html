Understanding Bone Health

Bone Density Conditions

Learn about the three bone density classifications our AI model detects from knee X-ray images.

🦴

Osteoporosis

HIGH RISK

A condition where bones become weak and brittle, making them more susceptible to fractures from minor falls or everyday stresses.

Common Causes

  • Age-related bone loss (post-menopausal women)
  • Calcium and Vitamin D deficiency
  • Hormonal imbalances (low estrogen/testosterone)
  • Sedentary lifestyle and lack of weight-bearing exercise
  • Prolonged corticosteroid medication use

Symptoms

  • Bone fractures from minor trauma
  • Gradual loss of height over time
  • Stooped or hunched posture
  • Back pain from vertebral compression fractures
  • Reduced bone density visible on X-rays
⚡

Osteopenia

MEDIUM RISK

A precursor to osteoporosis where bone density is lower than normal but not yet low enough to be classified as osteoporosis.

Common Causes

  • Natural aging process starting after age 30
  • Family history of low bone density
  • Insufficient calcium intake in diet
  • Smoking and excessive alcohol consumption
  • Certain medications (anti-seizure, steroids)

Symptoms

  • Often asymptomatic (silent condition)
  • Mild joint stiffness or discomfort
  • Increased susceptibility to fractures
  • Subtle changes in bone texture on imaging
  • May progress to osteoporosis if untreated
✅

Normal Bone Density

HEALTHY

Bones with adequate mineral density and structural integrity, indicating good skeletal health and low fracture risk.

Maintaining Bone Health

  • Regular weight-bearing and resistance exercises
  • Adequate calcium intake (1000-1200mg daily)
  • Sufficient Vitamin D (600-800 IU daily)
  • Avoiding smoking and excessive alcohol
  • Regular bone density screenings after age 50

Healthy Indicators

  • Dense, well-structured bone tissue on X-rays
  • Strong joint alignment and spacing
  • No signs of bone thinning or erosion
  • Healthy trabecular bone patterns
  • Normal cortical bone thickness
